## Diagnosis

**A strong start, but the volume asymmetry with the English version is striking:**

| | Impressions | Position | CTR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English (`web-statistics-morocco-2026`) | 3,570 | 6.06 | 0.06% |
| French (`statistiques-web-maroc-2026`) | 150 | 5.29 | **1.33%** |

The English article draws **24× the impressions**. Both rank similarly (5–6). The difference is
addressable market: English-language research demand for Moroccan digital statistics — journalists,
analysts, international agencies — vastly exceeds French-language demand for the same figures.

**But the French version converts 22× better.** 2 clicks from 150 impressions versus 2 from 3,570.

That contrast is worth understanding rather than dismissing as small numbers. A plausible reading:
English searchers want a *figure* and take it from the snippet; French searchers are more likely to
be local businesses wanting *context*, and they click through. If so, the French version is closer
to a lead-generating asset than the English one — the opposite of what the impression counts
suggest.

**Zero query coverage means this is a hypothesis, not a finding.** 150 impressions is below the
threshold where Google reveals much.

**Unlike its English twin, this page shows no trailing-slash duplication** — only the unslashed URL
carries impressions. Worth preserving when the canonicalisation rule is applied.
